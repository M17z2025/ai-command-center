# PROVE YOU'RE HUMAN — Production Bible v1

## Format
- Main master: 16:9, 4K if source pipeline supports it, 24 fps.
- Runtime target: 29–31 minutes.
- Trailer: 60–90 sec, 16:9.
- Teaser: 20 sec, 9:16.
- Captions: English SRT + burned-in social derivatives only.
- Delivery audio: stereo master plus stems where supported.

## Visual identity
Grounded London realism. Near-future details should feel one software update ahead of the present, not science fiction.
- Palette: cool practical interiors, sodium/LED urban night, natural grey-blue dawn.
- Avoid neon cyberpunk clichés.
- UI should be sparse and legible.
- Faces must remain natural; preserve skin texture, asymmetry and age detail.
- Camera begins controlled and becomes less stable as Nia loses trust, then settles into wide human frames at dawn.

## Character continuity
### Nia Shah
34, South Asian British woman, dark brown eyes, dark hair tied back at start, progressively loosening through story.
Wardrobe: charcoal trousers, black ankle boots, cream top, dark green overshirt/light jacket.
Prop: slim black smart ring on right index finger.
Performance: contained; emotion shows through breath, jaw tension and delayed reactions rather than melodrama.

### Ravi Shah
30, South Asian British man, similar family resemblance to Nia without looking like twins.
Wardrobe: navy bomber jacket, grey hoodie, dark jeans.
Prop: silver analogue watch with cracked black leather strap, worn on left wrist.
Performance: warm, impulsive, nervous humour.

### EVE
Voice only. Warm neutral British delivery. No humanoid avatar.
Motif: one restrained white pulse on devices.
Performance rule: never sinister. Calmness becomes unsettling only because of context.

### Mara Voss
40s, practical fraud-network operator.
Wardrobe: dark waterproof shell, neutral trousers.
No theatrical villain styling.

## Shot strategy by scene

### Scene 1
- Open in black on breath before first image.
- 50mm/65mm equivalent close visual language for call and Nia.
- Locked medium on Nia during EVE's manipulation verdict.
- Macro insert: watch, blood-like stain, handwritten note.
- Title on hard cut after EVE answers "I'm here."

### Scene 2
- TV replay reflected across Nia's face rather than full UI.
- Slow push-in during "tell me what you don't."
- Use practical lamp and dark negative space.

### Scene 3
- Office wides with Nia isolated in architecture.
- Tight inserts on waveform, paper notes, reflection clue.
- Printer should become uncanny through sound and framing, not horror lighting.
- End scene on purposeful handheld movement as Nia leaves.

### Scene 4
- Bus: compressed city reflections across glass.
- Storage: handheld but controlled, flashlight/practical motivated.
- Mara appears in a simple two-shot / over-shoulder exchange.
- Midpoint ends in still close-up on Nia after "How do you know?"

### Scene 5
- Minimal movement; let dialogue and revelation carry tension.
- Police blue flicker remains background texture only.
- End on Nia moving into rear stairwell.

### Scene 6
- 35mm close handheld through concrete corridor.
- No score during mechanical gate attempt.
- Physical struggle with manual release should feel tactile and human.
- Replica-watch reveal: three inserts only: Ravi's wrist / Nia's palm / engraved back.
- Hold on Nia before "Revoke every delegated permission."

### Scene 7
- Static 35mm/50mm wide at dawn.
- Natural city ambience.
- Close-up only for recovered voicemail screen.
- Final image is siblings walking away while camera remains.

## Generation prompt principles
Every visual prompt must include:
- exact character continuity descriptors;
- wardrobe/prop lock;
- location and time;
- emotional beat;
- camera framing + movement;
- realistic skin and lighting;
- "grounded contemporary London, no cyberpunk neon, no humanoid AI, no extra fingers, no text artifacts, no duplicated people".

Never rely on model-generated readable signage for plot-critical text. Composite or overlay necessary text in edit.

## Voice plan
Nia: British, London/SE England neutral contemporary accent; controlled, mid-low register.
Ravi: British, similar regional background; faster rhythm, warmer.
EVE: British neutral; calm, clear, never breathy/seductive/robotic.
Mara: understated British or European-in-London accent; no caricature.

Dialogue generation should be performed in clean isolated takes. Do not bake room reverb into voice generation; add in mix.

## Sound design
Motifs:
- EVE white-pulse cue: soft two-part synthetic tick, under 500 ms.
- Manipulated call: subtle codec modulation only.
- Transport clue: original three-note accessibility-style chime, newly composed.
- Printer mechanism amplified slightly.
- London beds: rain, bus HVAC, distant rail, station ventilation, tunnel hum.
- Silence is a designed element at EVE shutdown and final voicemail.

## Music
Sparse score only:
- Act 1: low sub pulse + processed piano harmonics.
- Act 2: asymmetric electronic rhythm, never trailer bombast.
- Storage reveal: remove percussion.
- Tunnel rescue: score drops out.
- Dawn: no score until optional single sustained organic tone after voicemail, then silence.
All music must be original or cleared for commercial release.

## Edit rules
- Cut on information change, not every line.
- Hold reactions 6–12 frames longer than conventional social-video pacing.
- Avoid overusing glitch transitions.
- No montage that obscures causality.
- Keep EVE mostly off-screen so audience focuses on Nia's dependence.
- Target dialogue scenes at 2.5–4.5 sec average shot length; suspense inserts can be shorter.
- Use J-cuts for EVE to make it feel omnipresent.

## VFX / compositing
- Device screens tracked cleanly in post.
- White-pulse motif added in post.
- No fake news broadcasts or real-person deepfakes.
- Replica-watch matching should be achieved through prop continuity, not text explanation.
- Remove obvious generation artifacts: hands, earrings, teeth, signage, reflections, shadow mismatches.

## QC gate
Must pass:
1. Character face continuity across all recurring scenes.
2. Wardrobe continuity.
3. Ravi watch continuity.
4. Nia smart-ring continuity.
5. No impossible hand/prop changes.
6. Lip-sync within acceptable tolerance.
7. Dialogue intelligible on phone/laptop speakers.
8. EVE level consistent across scenes.
9. No copyrighted logos/characters unintentionally prominent.
10. No misleading claim that real people/events depicted are authentic.
11. Captions manually reviewed against final audio.
12. Final synthetic-media disclosure and credits included where platform policy requires.
