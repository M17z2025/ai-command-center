# THE LINE — Book & Screen Franchise Architecture

## Strategy
Write the story first as a sequence of novels. Each book establishes deeper internal character, business mechanics, relationships and world-building. The television series then adapts the novels rather than inventing a new plot independently.

## Proposed novel cycle

### BOOK ONE — THE SHOP
M is a mid-30s British-Indian mobile-phone shop owner with Rhea, Asha and Atlas at home.
The business is under pressure, but M proves unusually good at solving other people's commercial problems.
A customer introduction pulls him into a failing business situation that appears ordinary but connects to a larger logistics/finance network.
The book ends with M earning his first meaningful consultancy fee and discovering that access is more valuable than stock.

Screen adaptation: Seasons 1–2.

### BOOK TWO — THE FIXER
M has multiple revenue streams and a reputation among SME owners as someone who can solve difficult problems.
He enters distressed-company transactions, procurement and logistics.
His first serious enemies appear.
He begins travelling internationally.
The title "Bossman" starts as a joke from people around him, then gradually sticks.

Screen adaptation: Seasons 3–4.

### BOOK THREE — THE CONCESSION
M is introduced to African mining opportunities.
He initially misunderstands the sector and pays for those mistakes.
He learns licensing, geology, due diligence, local partnership structures and mineral trading.
A major Zambia transaction changes his scale.

Screen adaptation: Seasons 5–6.

### BOOK FOUR — THE NETWORK
M's companies now connect Europe, Africa and the Gulf.
Banks, lawyers, miners, commodity buyers, governments and criminal interests collide around the same deals.
M becomes a genuine power broker.
His family life becomes increasingly difficult to isolate from his reputation.

Screen adaptation: Seasons 7–8.

### BOOK FIVE — BOSSMAN
M has reached the position the earlier books promised.
People fear crossing him and compete for his approval.
The central question is no longer whether he can make it.

It is whether the man who built the empire can still control it — and whether Rhea and Asha recognise the person he has become.

Screen adaptation: Seasons 9–10 or a concluding feature/limited series.

## Adaptation principle
The books are canon.

The screen adaptation may compress or combine events but cannot contradict the established character history, business evolution, family relationships or major outcomes without a deliberate canon revision.

## Tone
Business rise saga + crime thriller + family drama + international mining/political thriller + dry humour.

The books should make business negotiations and deal mechanics dramatic without turning them into lectures.
