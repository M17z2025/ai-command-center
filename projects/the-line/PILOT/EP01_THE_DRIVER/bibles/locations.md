# THE LINE — Location & Production Design Bible v1

## LOC-PATEL-GARAGE
East London converted industrial unit. EV chargers, lift, battery diagnostic bench, tool chests, cable reels, tyre stacks, scuffed concrete, fingerprints, mixed old/new equipment, small office mezzanine. Warm human clutter; not a showroom.

## LOC-PATEL-HOME
Modest East London family home. Practical kitchen, family photos, lived-in surfaces, Atlas bed/feeding area, soft warm lighting.

## LOC-VALE-PRIVATE
Contemporary understated wealth. Stone, dark timber, soft indirect lighting, curated art, excellent acoustics. No gold excess.

## LOC-FOOTBALL
Community ground in East London. Worn but cared for. Chain-link, dugout, cones, bibs, parents, small stand, floodlights.

## LOC-NOAH-HOTEL
Business hotel room converted into evidence base. Neutral carpet, generic desk, taped maps, laptop, printed manifests, temporary feel.

## LOC-HAWTHORNE
Essex estate: heritage house, outbuildings, paddocks, utility yard, hedgerows, early autumn foliage. Attractive but financially pressured; maintenance backlog visible.

## LOC-BLACKTHORN
Modern logistics operations floor: glass, matte black/grey surfaces, practical LED, large operational screens, freight data—not sci-fi holograms.

## LOC-DOCKLANDS
London port/industrial roads: containers, gantries, service lanes, wet tarmac, sodium/LED pools, realistic signage and road furniture.

## Reuse rule
Build fixed master references for every location from 5+ angles: exterior day/night, interior wide, two dialogue angles, detail insert.
