# THE MAN THAT NEVER WAS — Novel Quality Gate

## Purpose
Prevent another treatment/screenplay draft from being mislabeled as a finished novel.

## Automatic fail conditions
- Default paragraph cadence is one sentence / one beat / one camera image.
- Dialogue occupies the majority of pages without sufficient narrative cognition.
- Scenes routinely begin and end like screenplay sequences.
- Narrative uses camera-like verbs instead of lived point of view.
- M's intelligence is demonstrated mainly by after-the-fact exposition.
- Kay exists mainly as a domestic cutaway.
- Jay exists mainly to execute orders.
- Violence lacks emotional/social consequences.
- Chapters are short because they are episode beats rather than complete dramatic units.
- Business/mining material is explained rather than dramatised through conflict and decision.

## Required literary characteristics
### Point of view
Close third-person centred primarily on M, with limited intentional departures only when dramatically justified.

### Scene depth
Major scenes must include:
- objective;
- pressure;
- social power;
- internal judgement;
- sensory environment;
- subtext;
- reversal or irreversible choice;
- aftermath where material.

### Character depth
M must sometimes misread, lose, regret or need another person's insight.
Kay must influence decisions and emotional reality.
Jay must have independent judgement and history with M.

### Prose
Narrative paragraphs should vary naturally in length.
Short lines are used for emphasis, not as default screenplay rhythm.
Description must reveal class, tension, character or information rather than decorate the page.

### Crime world
Use dramatic consequence, loyalty, rumour, fear and negotiation.
Do not turn criminal activity into instructional procedural detail.

### Long arc
Book One establishes M's first meaningful reputation and the bridge toward Africa/mining/ALMANAC.
It must not make him globally dominant too early.

## Acceptance evidence
- word count;
- chapter count;
- 5 random chapter prose samples;
- dialogue-to-narration spot check;
- continuity report;
- critic report with PASS;
- final editorial report.
