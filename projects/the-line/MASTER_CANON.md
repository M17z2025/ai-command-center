# THE LINE — Master Canon v2

## Protagonist
### BOSSMAN "M"
British-Indian man in his mid-30s.

"M" is the name most people know him by. The title **Bossman** is earned gradually over years; it must not feel self-appointed at the beginning.

At the start he is:
- intelligent but underestimated;
- commercially sharp;
- good at reading people;
- loyal to a very small circle;
- cautious about debt and dependency;
- not a gangster;
- not wealthy;
- not feared;
- running a modest mobile-phone retail/repair business.

His long arc is the spine of the books and television series:
**shopkeeper -> fixer -> consultant -> operator -> international businessman -> mining dealmaker -> power broker -> feared boss.**

The transformation must take several seasons and multiple novels.

## Home life
M is married.

### RHEA
M's wife. Intelligent, emotionally perceptive and far less impressed by status than everyone around him. She understands that M is ambitious long before he admits it to himself.

### ASHA
Their daughter. Young enough at the start for the audience to watch her grow as M's world changes.

M's wife and daughter are his emotional centre.

He keeps his wider family at a distance. He does not routinely mix business, extended family and home life.

Both of M's parents are deceased before the story begins. Their absence matters psychologically but should not become melodrama. Pieces of his childhood and relationship with them are revealed slowly.

## Atlas
Atlas remains M's Doberman:
- gentle with Rhea and Asha;
- extremely bonded to M;
- protective without being uncontrollably aggressive;
- a recurring visual symbol of trust and loyalty.

## Starting business
M begins with a **mobile-phone shop**, not a garage.

The shop should feel real:
- handset sales;
- repairs;
- screen replacements;
- accessories;
- SIMs/contracts;
- data transfer;
- small-business IT help;
- trade-ins;
- cashflow pressure;
- difficult customers;
- supplier relationships.

His commercial intelligence is visible here first.

Customers begin asking him for help outside phones:
- sourcing stock;
- sorting difficult suppliers;
- negotiating invoices;
- recovering stalled deals;
- introductions;
- solving operational problems.

This evolves naturally into business consultancy and dealmaking.

## Business expansion
M gradually moves into:
1. mobile retail/repairs;
2. multiple shops / device distribution;
3. SME troubleshooting and consultancy;
4. logistics and procurement;
5. distressed business opportunities;
6. international commodity introductions;
7. Africa-facing business;
8. mining-sector consultancy / representation;
9. mining transactions and strategic interests;
10. a diversified group.

M must earn capability in each stage. He does not magically become a mining expert overnight.

## Africa / mining
Africa is a major long-term strand, not a quick exotic backdrop.

The series may move through Zambia and other African mining jurisdictions as M's commercial network grows.

Mining storylines should include credible:
- licences;
- geology;
- due diligence;
- JV structures;
- local partners;
- government interfaces;
- community relationships;
- finance;
- logistics;
- mineral buyers;
- fraud risk;
- political risk;
- competing interests.

## Power
M eventually becomes feared because:
- he remembers everything;
- he pays attention to details others ignore;
- he delivers;
- he protects loyal people;
- he does not make empty threats;
- he understands business structures and leverage;
- he builds networks across countries and industries.

He should not become feared because he suddenly becomes gratuitously violent.

## Family boundary
M deliberately keeps Rhea and Asha outside his commercial world as much as possible.

This creates tension:
the more powerful he becomes, the more difficult it is to keep those worlds separate.

## Long-form rule
No rapid-rise montage that turns M into a major boss within one season.

The transformation should require:
- setbacks;
- bad investments;
- betrayal;
- debt/cashflow pressure;
- mentors;
- rivals;
- mistakes;
- African travel;
- regulatory problems;
- reputational damage;
- hard-won wins;
- personal sacrifice.

The final Bossman must feel earned.
