# THE MAN THAT NEVER WAS — Crime Drama Style Bible

## Narrative grammar
- Open late in situations: reader enters after the problem has started.
- Reveal context through action and dialogue.
- Frequently show consequence before cause, then reveal M anticipated it.
- Use parallel storylines that collide.
- End chapters on decisions, reversals or discoveries.
- Let M's silence carry meaning.

## Dialogue
Short, specific, British, layered.

Characters rarely say exactly what they mean.

Avoid:
- monologues explaining criminal organisations;
- exposition disguised as conversation;
- generic threats;
- everyone speaking with the same voice.

M:
few words, often questions.

Jay:
direct, dry, operational.

Kay:
warm, intelligent, able to cut through M's mythology instantly.

Kerr:
polished, experienced, increasingly irritated that M does not behave according to fixer convention.

## Humour
Humour comes from:
- understatement;
- timing;
- ordinary problems during extraordinary situations;
- Jay and M's lifelong familiarity;
- Kay treating feared men as ordinary guests if they enter her home.

Never turn danger into slapstick.

## Action
Action is:
- sudden;
- confusing at first;
- physically consequential;
- short enough to feel real;
- used to change power.

Avoid endless interchangeable gunfights.

The most memorable action scene should often be the one M prevents.

## M's dramatic trick
The reader must sometimes think:
'M has made a mistake.'

Then later discover he allowed other people to believe that because he needed them to move.

Do not overuse.
If every twist is 'M knew everything', he becomes unbelievable.

He must sometimes:
- miscalculate;
- lose money;
- trust the wrong person;
- be emotionally wrong;
- be saved by Jay;
- need Kay's perspective.

That makes the moments where he is two steps ahead satisfying.

## Violence and underworld
Criminality can involve drugs, weapons, gangs and conflict because those are part of the fictional world.

M's dramatic function is primarily:
- mediator;
- fixer;
- strategist;
- broker of outcomes.

The story should focus on character, consequence, power and moral compromise rather than instructional detail about committing crimes.

## Visual identity for screen adaptation
- premium British realism;
- Essex/London contrast;
- quiet-luxury wardrobe for M;
- lived-in gang environments rather than fantasy criminal palaces;
- strong night exteriors;
- restaurants, social clubs, private rooms, warehouses, airport lounges, mining sites, government offices;
- violence contrasts with calm domestic scenes with Kay and Asha.

## Series hook
Every episode should leave viewers with one question:
What does M know that everyone else doesn't?
