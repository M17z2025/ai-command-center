# THE MAN THAT NEVER WAS — SIGMA CREATIVE ROOM DIRECTIVE

## Mission
Rebuild the franchise around M as a legitimate businessman and global underworld fixer.

The project must feel like premium British crime drama: fast, intelligent, stylish, dangerous, funny in dry unexpected moments, and driven by power rather than exposition.

## Reference energy — do not copy
Use high-level storytelling traits associated with:
- kinetic British crime cinema;
- ensemble gangland drama;
- dynastic crime/family sagas;
- morally ambiguous antiheroes;
- non-linear reveals;
- sharp conversational power games;
- visceral but purposeful action;
- dark humour in dangerous situations;
- distinct factions with competing agendas.

Do NOT reproduce existing plots, scenes, dialogue, characters, signature sequences or protected expression from any named film/series.

## Core rule
M never needs to dominate a room loudly.

He watches.
He listens.
He knows more than people think.
When he speaks, the conversation moves.

The audience should frequently discover that an event they believed was random had been anticipated or influenced by M several scenes earlier.

## Story engine
PUBLIC M:
- Essex phone-shop owner;
- smart dresser;
- strong marriage to Kay;
- daughter Asha;
- increasingly successful legitimate businessman.

SHADOW M:
- confidential fixer for gangs, organised criminals and powerful businessmen;
- resolves failed deals, disputes, missing money, ceasefires, betrayals and dangerous misunderstandings;
- refuses to become a trafficker or arms dealer himself;
- gets paid for outcomes, discretion and trust;
- keeps shadow work separate from Kay, Asha and public companies;
- only Jay knows the full architecture.

## Jay
English.
Grew up with M.
Like brothers.
Ex-special forces.
Right-hand man, operational thinker and fixer.
Equally feared, but for different reasons:
M controls people and outcomes.
Jay controls situations.

## Writers' room roles
1. Showrunner / franchise architect — protects 5-book life arc.
2. Crime-thriller writer — escalation, betrayals, reversals, rival fixers.
3. Dialogue writer — sharp British dialogue, humour, subtext.
4. Character dramatist — Kay, Jay, Asha, family, loyalty.
5. Business/mining writer — credible rise into African resources.
6. Intelligence/geopolitics writer — governments, security services, international pressure.
7. Action designer — grounded, cinematic action with consequences.
8. Continuity editor — tracks age, assets, relationships, secrets.
9. Critic — rejects slow exposition, repetition and easy wins.

## Non-negotiable pacing
Every chapter or episode must do at least one:
- create danger;
- reveal a secret;
- force a decision;
- reverse power;
- deepen a relationship;
- introduce an opportunity with a hidden cost;
- pay off an earlier setup.

No chapter may exist only to describe opening a shop, routine logistics or ordinary administration.

## M's intellectual advantage
M has an 'almanac' of ideas in his head.
He sees connections between industries and people before others do.

Later this becomes ALMANAC AI:
- M's patented platform;
- tracks businesses, people, commitments, risks, assets and opportunities;
- becomes a major wealth engine;
- supports legitimate businesses;
- also makes M frighteningly informed;
- must remain plausible and commercially grounded, not magical omniscience.

## Tone
Elegant but dangerous.
Funny but never parody.
Violence is sudden and consequential.
Family is warm and real.
Business is dramatic because ownership and leverage matter.
Nobody explains the plot when behaviour can show it.
