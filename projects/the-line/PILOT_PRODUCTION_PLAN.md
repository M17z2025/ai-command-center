# THE LINE — Pilot Production Plan

## Production target
Create a finished 28–32 minute AI pilot, S01E01 **The Driver**, that can also serve as the opening chapter of an 8 x 30-minute series.

## Department decisions

### Writers Room
The pilot must be emotionally understandable without exposition overload. Mitz Patel is the audience anchor. Every other storyline must either threaten, assist, reveal, or foreshadow Mitz's rise.

### Casting
Use original faces, not celebrity lookalikes. Primary cast should visibly reflect modern Britain. Character silhouettes must be immediately distinguishable in low light and action scenes.

### Wardrobe / Costume
Mitz: practical workshop layers, contemporary trainers/boots, restrained palette; upgrades only gradually.
Kai: more fashion-forward, impulsive, streetwear influenced.
Marcus: quiet-luxury tailoring, modern rather than old-school mob costume.
Sofia: low-profile premium professional clothing that works for surveillance/action.
Tommy: credible modern football training wear; warm and accessible.
Ellie: contemporary rural-estate functional clothing, not period-country caricature.
Noah: minimal, durable, low-logo tactical-civilian wardrobe.

### Hair / Makeup
Create immutable continuity records per character: haircut, facial hair, skin details, jewellery, scars, injury state, dirt/blood continuity and time-of-day state.

### Locations
Core reusable sets:
- Patel Performance — East London industrial unit; electric conversion/performance workshop.
- Patel family home — modest, lived-in, warm.
- Vale residence/private club — contemporary understated wealth.
- Community football ground — multicultural East London.
- Hawthorne Estate — Essex countryside; heritage property under financial pressure.
- Blackthorn warehouse — high-security logistics environment.
- London Docklands/port-industrial roads — pilot action environment.
- Neutral intelligence/interview rooms — reusable.

### Vehicles — EV-first
Hero action vehicles should be current high-performance EVs or fictionalised equivalents based on the same architecture.
- Mitz hero car: high-performance electric saloon/GT; instant torque and understated exterior.
- Kai: modified electric performance hatch/crossover.
- Marcus: premium luxury electric saloon/SUV.
- Sofia: anonymous fast electric executive car.
- Blackthorn: electric commercial vans + premium EV SUVs.
- Hawthorne: electric 4x4 / utility vehicle where plausible.
Vehicle choreography must respect battery mass, regenerative braking, silence/tyre noise, thermal limits and realistic charging/logistics.

### Stunts
Chases should use EV-specific behaviour rather than pretending EVs are petrol cars:
- instant launch torque;
- strong low-speed acceleration;
- tyre/suspension/road noise as key sound elements;
- low centre of gravity;
- believable heavy-vehicle momentum;
- charging/range only when plot-relevant.
Keep violence intense but within a 15 classification target.

### Sound
No fake V8 soundtrack for EV hero cars. Build excitement with inverter/drivetrain whine, tyres, suspension load, wind, impacts, road texture, cabin vibration and designed bass. Each lead receives a musical motif.

### Music
Original score families:
- Mitz: UK garage / grime / drum-and-bass pulse.
- Marcus: modern soul / restrained jazz / luxury hip-hop.
- Blackthorn: industrial electronic pressure.
- Sofia/Noah: minimal electronic/military texture.
- Tommy/community: warmer indie/soul instrumentation.
- Finale/end credits: original song built around the theme "Everybody Has a Line".

## Calendar / News / Radio Continuity

### Series clock
- Every episode advances exactly **7 calendar days**.
- Season One therefore covers approximately **8 consecutive weeks**.
- The production bible must record a canonical in-story date for every episode and scene.
- Weather, sunrise/sunset, clothing layers, foliage, school terms, football calendar, traffic, public events and seasonal behaviour must match the story week.

### News and radio
- Background television, radio, podcasts, phone alerts, newspapers and public screens must be relevant to the specific story week.
- News should help make the world feel alive and may foreshadow plot developments, but must never overwhelm the principal story.
- Where real-world news is used, it must be contemporaneous to the in-story date and independently verified during episode production.
- Fictional local news may be used for Blackthorn, crime, business and community storylines, but must be clearly fictional and must not falsely depict real people or organisations as involved in fictional wrongdoing.
- Radio music, presenters and station branding require rights clearance unless created as original fictional equivalents.
- Recurring fictional station/news brands should be created so we can control continuity and avoid unnecessary licensing.

### Recommended Season One calendar
Unless superseded by the owner, anchor S01E01 in **late September 2026** and move forward one week per episode, taking the season through **mid-November 2026**.
This gives visible seasonal progression: early-autumn evenings -> colder/wetter October -> darker November, supporting the tone and wardrobe evolution.


## Anti-AI Finish / Photorealism Standard

The final episode must read as a professionally photographed live-action drama, not synthetic AI footage.

### Mandatory visual rules
- Natural skin texture: pores, fine lines, asymmetry, subtle blemishes; never waxy/plastic faces.
- Natural hair: flyaways, strand breakup, realistic movement and edge detail.
- Hands, teeth, ears, eyes and jewellery must be anatomically consistent and checked shot-by-shot.
- Clothing must show believable folds, stitching, wear, gravity and material response.
- Environments must contain controlled imperfection: scuffs, fingerprints, dust, clutter, tyre marks, weathering and lived-in detail.
- Avoid hyper-glossy surfaces, excessive bloom, oversharpening, fake depth-of-field and over-saturated cinematic presets.
- Camera language must feel physically operated: motivated handheld, tripod, dolly, vehicle rig, crane or drone; no impossible floating-camera motion.
- Lenses, focal depth, motion blur, shutter feel and exposure must remain physically plausible.
- Reflections, mirrors, glass, number plates, signage, shadows and contact points require continuity review.
- Dialogue shots must preserve identity, eyelines, lip sync and micro-expressions.
- Crowd/background people must be deliberately limited and checked to avoid duplicated/warped figures.

### Editorial rules
- Prefer shorter high-quality shots over longer unstable generations.
- Cut away before model degradation becomes visible.
- Use inserts, reaction shots, details, atmosphere and sound to hide generation joins naturally.
- No shot is approved solely because it is technically complete; it must pass the 'would a viewer assume this was filmed?' test.

### Sound realism
- Dialogue recorded/treated with consistent room acoustics.
- Realistic room tone, clothing rustle, footsteps, traffic, wind and environmental beds.
- EVs use authentic-feeling tyre, wind, suspension and drivetrain textures rather than fake petrol engines.
- Music must not mask weak dialogue or unrealistic ambience.

### Final QC gate
Every shot receives PASS / REGENERATE / REPAIR against:
1. identity continuity;
2. anatomy;
3. physics;
4. lighting/shadows;
5. materials;
6. environment continuity;
7. lip sync;
8. camera plausibility;
9. sound realism;
10. visible AI artefacts.

Any obvious AI artefact blocks the final master.


## Generation Provider Policy

### Primary production route
THE LINE must be produced through the open-source ALISHA/Sigma movie pipeline:
- ComfyUI
- Wan / Wan2.x
- LTX-Video / LTX-AV
- AnimateDiff where appropriate
- Wav2Lip
- Kokoro / Chatterbox
- ACE-Step
- Real-ESRGAN / GFPGAN
- Blender
- FFmpeg

### Runway policy
Runway is NOT the primary production engine and is not required for completion.
It may only be used as:
- optional visual reference generation;
- optional benchmark/fallback for a difficult shot;
- non-blocking comparison against the open-source pipeline.

No production milestone may depend on a paid Runway plan.
The film must remain buildable without Runway.
