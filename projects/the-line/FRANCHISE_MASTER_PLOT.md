# THE LINE — Franchise Master Plot v1

## Core premise
At 30, M is an Essex-raised British-Indian businessman with no formal qualifications, a small chain of mobile-phone shops, a strong marriage to Kay, daughter Asha, and lifelong friend Jay.

Over roughly fifty years, M builds two interconnected worlds:

1. A legitimate pan-African mining, resources, energy and infrastructure empire.
2. A deniable underworld fixer network whose power rests on relationships, stories, favours, fear and Jay's ability to solve difficult problems.

The public knows the businessman.
The underworld knows the fixer.
Governments know both exist.
Nobody knows the full truth.

The central question is not simply whether M can become powerful.

It is whether he can keep the two worlds separate long enough to build something that survives him.

# BOOK ONE — THE SHOP
## Age range: 30–35
### Theme
Nobody knows who M is yet.

### Public story
M is running a small but growing mobile-phone chain in Essex. He is commercially gifted but stretched, recently without his mother, and still treated dismissively by wealthy relatives on his mother's side.

Customers and local businessmen increasingly ask him to solve problems unrelated to phones.

He discovers that judgement, negotiation and introductions are more valuable than handset margins.

M begins:
- business consultancy;
- buying and sourcing;
- distribution;
- distressed-business problem solving;
- taking small equity positions.

### Jay
Jay is already M's lifelong friend and has returned from military life.

At first Jay does not formally work for M, but he naturally becomes the person M trusts when a problem needs discretion and operational thinking.

### Shadow story
One of M's shops becomes caught between local criminal interests and a legitimate supplier/customer dispute.

M refuses to be intimidated.

Instead of fighting, he brokers a solution.

Later, one of the men who repeatedly threatened M disappears from the local scene.

Nobody knows why.

M never discusses it.

The first stories begin.

### Marcus Vale
Marcus notices M after seeing him solve a commercial problem that experienced professionals failed to resolve.

Marcus initially treats M as useful but socially insignificant.

Their relationship becomes mentor / client / rival in embryonic form.

### Major twist
M accepts advice from someone more established and nearly destroys part of his business.

He learns his first major rule:
**never confuse status with competence.**

### Ending
M completes his first meaningful business rescue and earns both a fee and a small equity stake.

At a meeting, somebody jokingly calls him:

"Bossman."

M tells them not to.

The audience understands the title has begun.

# BOOK TWO — THE FIXER
## Age range: 35–42
### Theme
Access becomes power.

### Public story
M expands beyond telecoms.

He acquires interests in:
- distribution;
- logistics;
- commercial property;
- distressed businesses;
- advisory work.

He learns that ownership matters more than fees.

His reputation becomes:
"Call M. He'll sort it."

### Shadow story
Business disputes increasingly bring him into contact with gangland figures, debt disputes and people who cannot use ordinary channels.

M becomes valuable because he can speak to both legitimate businessmen and people outside conventional business.

He never advertises this role.

Jay becomes his formal right-hand man.

### The first real moral compromise
A businessman M is advising is being threatened.

Legal methods are moving too slowly.

M asks Jay to "make sure it stops."

The threat stops.

M does not ask exactly how.

This becomes an important line crossed.

### Marcus relationship
Marcus increasingly relies on M but also tries to control him.

M eventually realises Marcus has been using M's reputation and relationships to repair Marcus's own failing deals.

M quietly restructures one transaction so that he ends up owning an asset Marcus expected to control.

Marcus realises M is no longer merely useful.

### Underworld mythology
A major criminal figure tries to force M to take a side in a dispute.

M refuses.

The attempt to pressure him fails.

The criminal figure later withdraws from several businesses.

Stories spread:
"People have tried M before."

### Africa seed
A logistics/procurement client has a serious problem connected to Zambia.

M is asked to help.

He initially says he knows nothing about African mining.

The response:
"You didn't know anything about half the businesses you fixed."

### Ending
M boards a flight to Zambia with Jay.

His first African chapter begins.

# BOOK THREE — THE CONCESSION
## Age range: 42–52
### Theme
M learns that connections are not the same as knowledge.

### Public story
M enters African business through logistics, procurement and commercial advisory work.

Mining opportunities follow.

Initially he makes mistakes:
- bad geological assumptions;
- weak counterparties;
- exaggerated licences;
- unreliable intermediaries;
- financing problems;
- deals that collapse during due diligence.

Rather than pretending to know everything, M builds a serious team:
- geologists;
- mining lawyers;
- local operators;
- financiers;
- mineral buyers;
- logistics specialists.

Zambia becomes an important early base.

### Mining progression
M moves from:
adviser -> introducer -> small investor -> JV participant -> mine equity holder.

His first major successful mining transaction transforms his financial scale.

### Shadow story
The mining world introduces M to a different kind of danger.

Some commercial problems involve people who use intimidation rather than lawyers.

Jay builds a wider network of trusted intermediaries.

M's name begins travelling internationally before M himself does.

### Major antagonist
A powerful businessman with both political and criminal relationships attempts to take control of a mining opportunity M has spent years building.

M appears to lose.

Then:
- financing moves;
- partners change sides;
- key documents surface;
- the rival's support begins evaporating.

The audience sees pieces of what M and Jay did but never the whole mechanism.

### Intelligence interest
An intelligence service begins looking at M.

Not because he is a spy, but because they cannot understand how a British businessman with no formal qualifications has relationships spanning miners, politicians, security figures, traders and underworld intermediaries.

### Major twist
Someone M believes is corrupt turns out to have been protecting the transaction from a larger fraud.

Someone M trusted is actually the person leaking information.

M learns another rule:
**the obvious villain is often not the dangerous one.**

### Ending
M closes his first transformative African resources deal.

He now owns something real.

The phone-shop owner has become a resources businessman.

But the closing scene shows two men discussing him in another country.

One says:
"Do you know M?"

The other replies:
"Everybody knows M."

# BOOK FOUR — THE NETWORK
## Age range: 52–65
### Theme
Power becomes international.

### Public story
M's group expands across Africa.

It now has interests in:
- gold;
- copper;
- cobalt;
- lithium;
- precious stones;
- platinum-group metals;
- logistics;
- processing;
- infrastructure;
- selected oil and energy projects.

M is no longer merely a dealmaker.

He is becoming an operator and owner.

Governments know him because his companies employ people, move capital and control strategic assets.

### State relationships
Ministers know M.
Military figures respect him.
Intelligence organisations monitor him.

Nobody can decide whether M is:
- a businessman;
- a fixer;
- a strategic intermediary;
- or all three.

### Underworld story
M is now used as a neutral fixer by powerful figures in several countries.

When two sides cannot meet, M can sometimes bring them into the same room.

The mythology becomes stronger than confirmed facts.

A later-era signature scene:
a violent confrontation is escalating.
Weapons appear.
M walks into the room.

Nobody tells him what is happening.

People simply start putting the weapons away.

An outsider asks:
"Why are they listening to him?"

Nobody answers.

### Major threat
For the first time, several enemies cooperate:
- a wealthy commercial rival;
- an underworld figure;
- a compromised executive inside M's group;
- an official who wants M removed from a strategic resource transaction.

They believe M's network can be attacked from several directions simultaneously.

### The attack
M faces:
- frozen transactions;
- hostile media;
- regulatory investigation;
- financing pressure;
- betrayal from inside;
- physical threats.

He refuses to panic.

The legitimate response is fought with:
lawyers, records, governance, financing and commercial alliances.

The shadow response is much less visible.

Several threats simply stop.

### Cost
M wins, but not cleanly.

Someone close to the wider organisation is lost.
Jay is forced to confront what his role has become.
Kay sees more than M realises, although their marriage remains strong.

### Ending
A foreign official refers to him as "Mr M."

A businessman corrects him:

"Bossman."

Nobody laughs anymore.

# BOOK FIVE — BOSSMAN
## Age range: 65–80s
### Theme
Building power is easier than deciding what happens to it.

### Public empire
M controls a major pan-African resources group.

His interests span:
- metals;
- precious metals;
- precious stones;
- oil;
- energy;
- mining services;
- processing;
- logistics;
- infrastructure.

He is known by governments, investors and major industry figures.

### Private power
His fixer reputation has become almost mythological.

People who have never met him know stories.

Some are true.
Some are false.
M no longer knows all of them himself.

That becomes dangerous.

### Central conflict
A younger generation begins using M's name without his permission.

People claim to act "for M."

Deals are forced.
Threats are made.
Money changes hands.

The underworld mythology M once allowed to grow is now capable of contaminating his legitimate empire.

At the same time:
- regulators scrutinise the group;
- intelligence services want clarity about his network;
- rivals believe age has made him vulnerable;
- succession inside the group becomes unavoidable.

### Asha
Asha is now an adult with her own life and views.

She must decide whether she wants any role in the empire.

M must decide whether legacy means giving her control or giving her freedom.

### Jay
Jay is older too.

Their relationship is still brother-like.

Jay finally challenges M:
"We built this so nobody could control us. Now you're trying to control what happens after you're gone."

### Final adversary
The final threat is not one gangster or businessman.

It is the collision of:
- M's legitimate empire;
- his shadow mythology;
- succession;
- old enemies;
- younger opportunists;
- governments and intelligence services that no longer tolerate ambiguity.

### Final choice
M chooses to institutionalise the legitimate group so it can survive without him.

He deliberately breaks the dependency between the company and his private fixer network.

Some shadow relationships remain personal, but nobody inherits "Bossman" as a job title.

### Final confrontation
A younger rival believes an elderly M is finished and engineers a confrontation.

M does not defeat him physically.

Instead the rival discovers that:
- his money has moved;
- his partners have withdrawn;
- his political protection has vanished;
- his own people no longer want the fight.

M enters the room.

The rival asks:
"How?"

M answers:
"You spent your life collecting power. I spent mine collecting people."

### Ending
M is in his 80s.

Kay is still beside him.

He visits the location of the original phone shop.

The modern resources group operates without needing him every day.

A young employee does not recognise him.

An older employee does.

The younger one asks:
"Who was that?"

The answer:

"That's M."

Cut/end.

# SCREEN ADAPTATION

Each novel becomes one television series.

Recommended format:
- 8 episodes per book/series;
- approximately 35–45 minutes per episode;
- five series total;
- each series retains the central book arc but expands secondary characters and international locations.

Series 1: THE SHOP
Series 2: THE FIXER
Series 3: THE CONCESSION
Series 4: THE NETWORK
Series 5: BOSSMAN

## FILM ONE — THE LINE: RISE
Adapts Books One and Two.

Phone shops -> consultancy -> ownership -> fixer reputation -> Jay formalises his role -> first Africa opportunity.

Final image:
M and Jay arriving in Africa.

## FILM TWO — THE LINE: EMPIRE
Adapts Books Three and most of Four.

M learns mining -> first major concession -> African expansion -> government/military/intelligence relationships -> international underworld reputation -> coordinated attempt to destroy him.

Final image:

## FILM THREE — THE LINE: BOSSMAN
Adapts the end of Book Four and Book Five.

M at full power -> pan-African resources empire -> mythology becoming dangerous -> succession -> Asha -> older Jay -> final attempt against M -> separation of legitimate legacy from shadow network.

Final image:
the old phone-shop location and the simple answer:
"That's M."

# Franchise rules
1. M's mining/resources empire is genuinely legitimate and commercially substantial.
2. The shadow fixer network is separate, deniable and informal.
3. M does not become powerful overnight.
4. Every major gain carries a cost, compromise or new risk.
5. Jay is M's brother in everything but blood, not a disposable henchman.
6. Kay and M remain a strong pair throughout the saga.
7. Asha grows across the story and represents M's legacy question.
8. M's full name is never required; he remains M.
9. Underworld fear grows mostly from stories rather than constant on-screen violence.
10. The older M becomes, the less he needs to speak.
